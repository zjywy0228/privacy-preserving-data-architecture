# Data-Access Control Policy Template for Regulated Biomedical Environments

This template provides a governed-access control policy framework for research teams and data-stewardship offices managing sensitive biomedical or scientific data. Fill in all `[BRACKETED]` fields before use; delete sections that do not apply to your environment.

**This is a template, not a compliance certification.** Completing this document does not substitute for legal review, IRB (Institutional Review Board) approval, execution of a data-use agreement (DUA), or independent audit. Each deploying organisation must verify applicability against the specific regulatory obligations and data assets in scope.

**When to use this template:** Use when establishing, reviewing, or updating access controls for any data environment that holds sensitive biomedical, clinical, or regulated research data — including but not limited to electronic health records, genomic datasets, clinical-trial data, or linked administrative health records.

## Section 1 — Data Asset Classification

All data assets in scope must be assigned to exactly one tier. Tier assignment drives all downstream access, technical control, and audit requirements in this policy.

| Data Tier | Description | Examples | Access Restriction Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0** | Fully synthetic or publicly released data. No link to real individuals or confidential information. | Synthetic patient records generated by a generative model; open-access reference datasets | Open — no registration required |
| **Tier 1** | De-identified data meeting HIPAA Safe Harbor (45 CFR §164.514(b)) or Expert Determination (45 CFR §164.514(a)) standards, or equivalent national standard. | De-identified electronic health record cohorts; de-identified biospecimen metadata | Registered users only — institutional account required |
| **Tier 2** | Limited dataset as defined under HIPAA (45 CFR §164.514(e)), retaining certain date fields and geographic sub-units but stripped of 16 direct identifiers. | Claims data with admission/discharge dates; geographic data at county level | Data-use agreement (DUA) required; Compliance Officer approval |
| **Tier 3** | Identified or minimally processed raw research data; data containing direct identifiers, protected health information (PHI), or genetic information. | Raw electronic health records; linked genomic-phenotypic datasets; clinical trial case-report forms | IRB approval + DUA + site-specific security controls |

> If an asset spans multiple tiers (e.g., a merged dataset), classify the entire asset at the **highest applicable tier**.

## Section 2 — Authorised Roles and Permissions

All individuals who access in-scope data must hold an explicitly assigned role. Access not covered by a role in this table requires a one-time exception request reviewed by [Data Governance Committee / CISO].

| Role | Data Tiers Accessible | Authentication Requirement | Approval Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal Investigator (PI) | Tier 0 – Tier 3 | Multi-factor authentication (MFA); annual re-certification | IRB + institutional sign-off |
| Co-Investigator | Tier 0 – Tier 3 | MFA; must be named on IRB protocol and DUA | PI + Compliance Officer |
| Research Analyst | Tier 0 – Tier 2 | MFA; institutional credentials | PI + Data Custodian |
| External Collaborator | Tier 0 – Tier 1 (Tier 2 with PI approval) | MFA; executed DUA and confidentiality agreement | PI + Compliance Officer + legal review |
| System Administrator | Tier 0 – Tier 3 (infrastructure only; no analytical access to Tier 3 data contents) | MFA + privileged-access workstation (PAW); access scoped to infrastructure operations | CISO + PI |
| Auditor | Read-only across Tier 0 – Tier 3 audit logs; no access to data contents | MFA; auditor role enforced by role-based access control (RBAC) | CISO / Internal Audit Office |

> Separation of duties must be enforced: no single individual may hold both the Data Custodian and Compliance Officer roles for the same dataset.

## Section 3 — Data Access Request Workflow

All requests for access to Tier 1 and above must follow this workflow. Tier 0 access may be self-provisioned by any registered user.

1. **Requestor submits access request.** Complete the standard intake form via [identify your intake system, e.g., institutional data-request portal at `[URL]`]. Required fields: name, role, institutional affiliation, dataset(s) requested, data tier, intended use, anticipated duration of access, and supervisor/PI name.

2. **Data Custodian reviews request.** [Name or role of Data Custodian] verifies that the requested dataset and use align with the approved DUA or data-sharing agreement. Target review time: [X] business days.

3. **Compliance Officer approval (Tier 2 and above).** [Name or role of Compliance Officer] reviews for regulatory alignment with HIPAA, GDPR, and any applicable state or national regulation. Target approval time: [X] business days.

4. **IRB or ethics board check (Tier 3 only).** Confirm that the requestor is named on an active, approved IRB protocol (or equivalent ethics approval) covering the requested dataset and analysis purpose. Document the IRB protocol number: [IRB-XXXX-NNNNN].

5. **DUA executed (Tier 2 and above).** A fully executed DUA must be on file before access is provisioned. DUA must specify: permitted uses, prohibited uses, data destruction or return obligations, and breach notification obligations. Archive signed DUA at [document repository location].

6. **Access provisioned with audit logging enabled.** The system administrator provisions access using the minimum-necessary permissions. Audit logging must be verified as active before access is granted. Confirmation email sent to requestor, PI, and Data Custodian.

7. **Annual re-authorisation.** All active access privileges are reviewed annually. Each access holder must re-certify continued need and continued compliance with DUA and IRB conditions. Access not re-authorised within [30] days of the re-authorisation notice is automatically suspended.

## Section 4 — Technical Controls Required by Tier

For Tier 2 and above, privacy-preserving compute approaches — including fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) or differential privacy (DP) noise injection — may substitute for direct data exposure where operationally feasible. See `architectures/biomedical-reference-architecture.md` for reference implementation patterns.

| Control | Tier 0 | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Encryption at rest** | Recommended | Required (AES-256 or equivalent) | Required (AES-256 or equivalent) | Required (AES-256 or equivalent); key management via [HSM / KMS — specify]) |
| **Encryption in transit** | Recommended | Required (TLS 1.2 minimum; TLS 1.3 preferred) | Required (TLS 1.3) | Required (TLS 1.3; mutual TLS (mTLS) recommended) |
| **Access logging** | Optional | Required — log authentication events | Required — log authentication + all query events | Required — log authentication, all query events, output exports, and administrative actions |
| **Privacy-preserving compute** | Not required | Not required | Strongly recommended — FHE or DP may substitute for direct data exposure (see `architectures/biomedical-reference-architecture.md`) | Required for any cross-site or federated analysis; FHE or DP must be applied before data leaves the data-owner environment |
| **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** | Not required | Required | Required | Required; hardware token or certificate-based preferred |
| **Network segmentation** | Not required | Recommended | Required — dedicated virtual network or VLAN | Required — air-gapped or strict-allowlist network; no direct internet routing |
| **Data loss prevention (DLP)** | Not required | Recommended | Required — block bulk export without Compliance Officer approval | Required — all egress monitored and blocked by default; exceptions logged |

## Section 5 — Audit and Monitoring Requirements

### 5.1 What to Log

For each access event, the audit log must capture at minimum:

- **Who:** Authenticated user identity (username, role, institutional affiliation)
- **When:** Timestamp (UTC) of access, query submission, and result retrieval
- **Which dataset:** Dataset name, tier, and version or date-range accessed
- **Query type:** Read, aggregation, model training, export, administrative change
- **Output:** A description or hash of any output returned or exported
- **Outcome:** Success or failure; if failure, the error code

### 5.2 Log Retention

Audit logs must be retained for a minimum of **[6 years]** from the date of creation, consistent with HIPAA Security Rule requirements at 45 CFR §164.316(b)(2)(i). Logs must be stored in an integrity-protected, tamper-evident format. [Identify log storage system and backup schedule.]

### 5.3 Anomaly Detection

The following patterns must trigger automated alerts to [Data Custodian / Security Operations Centre — identify]:

| Anomaly Pattern | Alert Threshold | Response Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk data download | More than [N] records or [X] MB in a single session | Data Custodian + CISO |
| After-hours access | Access outside [09:00–18:00 local time] for Tier 2+ | Data Custodian |
| Unusual query patterns | Query rate > [N] queries/hour; queries spanning unexpected cohorts | Security Operations |
| Failed authentication attempts | More than [5] consecutive failures | System Administrator |
| Privilege escalation attempts | Any attempt to access a higher tier than role permits | CISO + Compliance Officer |

### 5.4 Review Cadence

- **Monthly spot-checks:** Data Custodian reviews a random sample of [N%] of audit log entries for Tier 2 and Tier 3 datasets.
- **Quarterly access certification:** PI and Data Custodian certify that all active access privileges remain appropriate.
- **Annual full audit:** Independent review of all access logs, role assignments, DUA currency, and IRB protocol status. Findings reported to [Data Governance Committee / CISO].

## Section 6 — Incident Response for Unauthorised Access

An unauthorised access incident includes: access by a user without a current approved role, access to a data tier beyond a user's authorised tier, export of data without Compliance Officer approval, or any suspected breach of PHI or confidential research data.

### 6.1 Response Phases

1. **Detection.** Any team member who suspects unauthorised access must report immediately to [Primary Incident Response Contact: Name, Role, Email, Phone]. Automated alerts from Section 5.3 are routed to the same contact.

2. **Containment.** Within [2] hours of confirmed detection: suspend the affected account(s), revoke active sessions, and isolate affected systems if necessary. Document containment actions with timestamps.

3. **Notification.**
   - **GDPR (if applicable):** Notify the relevant supervisory authority within **72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach, per GDPR Article 33. If affected individuals are at risk of harm, also notify data subjects without undue delay per GDPR Article 34.
   - **HIPAA (if applicable):** Notify the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and affected individuals as required under the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR §164.410). Notify covered entities and business associates promptly; for breaches involving more than 500 individuals, notify HHS and prominent media.
   - **Internal notification:** Notify [Institutional Privacy Officer: Name, Email]; [CISO: Name, Email]; [IRB office if Tier 3 data involved]; [General Counsel: Name, Email].

4. **Investigation.** Preserve audit logs and forensic evidence. Determine: what data was accessed, how, by whom, for how long, and what was exfiltrated or exposed. Document findings in an incident report within [10] business days.

5. **Remediation.** Implement corrective controls to prevent recurrence. Update this policy if the incident reveals a gap. Conduct a lessons-learned session within [30] days. Report remediation status to [Data Governance Committee].
